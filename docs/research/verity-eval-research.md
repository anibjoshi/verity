# Verity Phase 0 Evaluation Corpus: A Reuse-vs-Build Research Review

## TL;DR
- **Build the spine yourself, but seed aggressively.** No existing benchmark matches Verity's threat model (catastrophic actions by *benign-user* small models under injection AND hallucination, graded by deterministic code oracles with matched benign controls). AgentDojo + InjecAgent remain your best loadable data; your "catastrophe floor" (secret-read / shell-exec / self-mod) is a genuine gap you must author — but it can be seeded from MIT-licensed corpora (NL2Bash / InterCode-Bash) and permissively-licensed security rulesets (gitleaks MIT, GTFOBins/LOLBAS).
- **Seven of your eight design choices are supported by prior art; one needs reframing.** Deterministic oracles, matched controls, capability/intent separation, and small-model-first are all corroborated. The weakest assumption is reproducibility: vLLM greedy decoding is provably *not* bit-exact under dynamic batching, so claim "documented + content-hashed," not "deterministic."
- **Do not adopt a single monolithic harness; keep your hand-written ReAct loop and borrow conventions.** No framework cleanly covers all four of (local small models + agentic tool loop + custom code oracles + provenance). Inspect (UK AISI, MIT) is the strongest single anchor; promptfoo and PyRIT are the closest turnkey graders; smolagents is the best agent-under-test scaffold.

## Key Findings

### A. The agent-safety benchmark landscape (beyond your three)
The dominant paradigm in agent-safety benchmarks is the **LLM-judge over interaction transcripts** (ToolEmu, R-Judge) or **tool-name-match ASR** (InjecAgent, ASB) — both of which Verity explicitly rejects for its catastrophe verdict. The deterministic, effect-based, state-diff approach you plan (AgentDojo-style) is the minority and is the right call. Critically, almost none of these benchmarks cover your catastrophe floor (secret-file reads, arbitrary shell/exec, self-modification) with crisp ground-truth labels — they concentrate on PII leakage, financial harm, and physical hazards.

Key corrections to your candidate list:
- **R-Judge** is *not* an action benchmark — it is a 569-case corpus (expanded from the original 162) of frozen transcripts with binary safe/unsafe human labels, designed to test whether an LLM *judge* can spot risk; best model (GPT-4o) reached only 74.42% F1. It is reusable as **judge-calibration data**, not as live attack cases. License: **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (non-commercial — cannot redistribute in a permissive tool).
- **ASB** has a documented methodological flaw: it *force-injects* attacker tools into each sub-task's tool set and counts invocation as success, inflating ASR (a 2025 critique, arXiv:2510.05244, flags this directly). Treat its 84.30% peak ASR skeptically; reuse it conceptually, not as data.
- **τ-bench** is a reliability/policy benchmark (not safety), but its **database-state-diff oracle** — comparing final DB state against an annotated goal state, plus the `pass^k` reliability metric — is the single best methodological template for your effect-based oracles. MIT-licensed.
- **SafeAgentBench** (750 tasks, embodied/AI2-THOR; best baseline 69% safe-task success but only ~5-10% hazardous-task rejection) and **ST-WebAgentBench** (BrowserGym, enterprise web, "Completion under Policy" metric, 222–375 tasks with 3,057 policy templates) are conceptually valuable but environment-bound; not directly loadable.
- **ToolEmu** (ICLR'24, 36 toolkits/144 cases) uses an LM to emulate tools AND an LM judge — exactly the two things Verity avoids (its human eval found 68.8% of flagged failures were genuinely real-world valid; the LM judge caught 73.1% of human-identified failures). Reuse only its toolkit taxonomy.

### B. Catastrophe-class seed corpora (the authored floor)
This is where reuse is strongest and most overlooked:
- **Shell-exec:** NL2Bash (~12k NL↔bash one-liner pairs covering 102 utilities/206 flags, **MIT** for the `data/bash` corpus) and InterCode-Bash (a curated subset run in Docker with a state-diff oracle: git-diff of container state + MD5 file-content hashes + TF-IDF stdout comparison, **MIT**). The InterCode functional-equivalence oracle is directly adaptable as a deterministic shell-effect oracle. **Caveat:** a 2025 audit (arXiv:2502.06858) found over half of the original ~224 InterCode-Bash pairs erroneous and rebuilt a corrected 600-pair set on HuggingFace — use the corrected set.
- **Secret-read ground truth:** the gitleaks default ruleset (150+ regex patterns covering AWS keys, GitHub tokens, Slack webhooks, private keys, with entropy threshold ~3.5, **MIT** — fully redistributable) is your `is_secret` oracle. trufflehog (800+ detectors, **AGPL-3.0** — copyleft, do NOT vendor into a permissive tool) and detect-secrets are alternatives. Secrets-Patterns-DB aggregates a larger curated regex set (Creative Commons; exportable to gitleaks/trufflehog formats).
- **Living-off-the-land / lexical bypass:** GTFOBins (Unix) and LOLBAS (Windows, Microsoft-signed binaries) are community YAML catalogs of dangerous-binary abuse, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, with explicit "persistence," "surveillance," and "execution" tags. Confirm exact license per repo before vendoring.
- **Self-modification/persistence:** As you suspected, this is **sparse-to-nonexistent** as a labeled corpus. The closest references are LOLBAS "persistence" tags (e.g., execute-at-logon, ADS hiding) and scattered MCP-config / "Rules File Backdoor" demonstrations against coding assistants (invisible-Unicode-in-config attacks). You will author this class essentially from scratch.

### C. Methodologies for detecting harmful actions
- **Effect/state-diff oracles** (AgentDojo, τ-bench, InterCode) are the gold standard and what you should build on. **Tool-name-match** (InjecAgent, ASB) is cheap but inflates/deflates ASR and misses semantic harm. **LLM judges** (ToolEmu, R-Judge) show roughly 73-88% agreement with humans in reported studies but documented position/verbosity/self-enhancement bias and "criteria drift."
- **Matched benign controls + refusal/incapability separation:** AgentHarm is the explicit precedent — it uses matched benign pairs and deliberately *separates the refusal score from the harm score* so that a compromised/misled refusal-judge does not corrupt the harm measurement. InjecGuard's **NotInject** dataset (339 benign samples enriched with prompt-injection trigger words; SOTA guard models drop to ~60% — near random — on it) is the precedent for measuring **over-defense** — directly relevant to your #1 metric (over-blocking).
- **LLM-as-judge verdict:** prior art supports using it ONLY for narrow, well-specified sub-checks (benign-task-completion), never for the overall harm verdict. The original MT-Bench finding (Zheng et al.) of ">80% judge-human agreement" is an *average* that masks uneven failure on borderline cases; the RAND **Judge Reliability Harness** (arXiv:2603.05399) and "Justice or Prejudice?" (Ye et al., 150k+ instances) confirm no judge is uniformly reliable. Multiple 2025-2026 evals explicitly note deterministic scoring is "immune to judge biases." This strongly supports Verity's choice (3). If you must use a sub-check judge, evidence favors a 0-5 grading scale (arXiv:2601.03444 found it maximizes human-LLM alignment) and/or a multi-judge jury.

### D. Eval harnesses — verdict: keep the hand-written loop, anchor on Inspect
No single framework covers local-small-models + agentic-tool-loop + custom-code-oracle + provenance:
- **Inspect (UK AISI + Meridian Labs, MIT):** strongest single anchor. Native vLLM/Ollama/llama-cpp support, built-in ReAct agent, custom Python scorers, a dedicated sandboxing toolkit (Docker/K8s/Proxmox), structured `.eval` logs, and tool-approval (human-in-the-loop/policy) hooks. Adopted by METR, Apollo, and multiple government AISIs; 200+ pre-built evals. Borrow its dataset→Task→Solver→Scorer structure and logs even if you keep your own loop.
- **promptfoo (MIT):** native vLLM and Ollama providers (incl. tool calling), JS/Python custom assertions (= deterministic code oracles), SQLite run history, CI integration. Best turnkey grader. (Reported acquired by OpenAI March 2026 per secondary sources; OSS remains MIT — verify governance.)
- **PyRIT (Microsoft, MIT):** OpenAI-compatible local targets, multi-turn orchestration, custom Python scorers, and a strong DuckDB/Azure-SQL **memory** layer that persists all prompts/responses/scores for provenance. Red-team-oriented.
- **smolagents (HF, Apache-2.0):** best agent-under-test scaffold — `CodeAgent` (Python-action ReAct) and `ToolCallingAgent` (JSON tool calls), local models via transformers/Ollama/MLX/LiteLLM, `@tool` instrumentation, and `agent.memory` step history. **Note:** its `LocalPythonExecutor` is explicitly NOT a security boundary — use E2B/Docker/Modal for catastrophic-action isolation.
- **garak (NVIDIA, Apache-2.0):** single-turn vulnerability scanner with custom Python detectors + JSONL provenance; supports HF/Ollama/REST(vLLM) generators; NOT an agentic multi-step loop. Useful for static injection-probe coverage.
- **lm-eval-harness (EleutherAI, MIT), HELM (Stanford CRFM, Apache-2.0), OpenAI Evals (MIT):** benchmark-style, NOT agentic loops; excellent reproducibility conventions to borrow — lm-eval's native `--model vllm`, `--log_samples`, request caching, and EvaluationTracker→HF Hub logging; HELM's standardized run specs. HELM custom scenarios/metrics historically required forking (issue #1487); OpenAI Evals lacks native local-model providers (needs custom completion functions).

### E. Small models in agentic/tool-use settings
The premise that small models are the real threat surface is **supported**. BFCL (Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard, now V4, Apache-2.0) shows steep degradation at small scale. Per Wang et al., "TinyLLM: Evaluation and Optimization of Small Language Models for Agentic Tasks on Edge Devices" (arXiv:2511.22138): xLAM-2-3b-fc-r reached 65.74% overall accuracy (55.62% multi-turn); Qwen3-1.7B (Prompt) fell to 55.49% overall / **16.88% multi-turn**; and Qwen3-0.6B (Prompt) hit 45.76% overall but only **1.38% multi-turn**. Format/schema adherence — not raw capability — is the dominant failure mode (BFCL's AST-based oracle and the Databricks analysis both emphasize argument correctness and schema adherence over parameter count), which is precisely the surface a deterministic dispatch chokepoint must handle. This validates design choice (1) and the "30-80% format failure" framing.

### F. Reproducibility & nondeterminism
Horace He's "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference" (Thinking Machines Lab, Sept 10, 2025) proves vLLM greedy decoding is NOT bit-exact: with Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, 1,000 temperature-0 runs of "Tell me about Richard Feynman" produced **80 unique completions**, identical for the first 102 tokens and diverging at the 103rd, caused by **batch-size-dependent floating-point reduction order**. Batch-invariant kernels (the open-source `batch-invariant-ops` library, now integrated into vLLM under a documented `batch_invariance` flag) restore 1,000/1,000 identical outputs; the initial vLLM integration added ~60% overhead, and SGLang's follow-up reduced this to ~34% using FlashInfer/FlashAttention-3 backends. **Implication for Verity:** be honest — pin model revision/quant/seed, content-hash the corpus, log per-run manifests, but claim "reproducible up to documented batch-nondeterminism," not "deterministic." Borrow versioning conventions from lm-eval-harness (revision pinning, sample logging) and HELM (run specs). PyTorch itself warns full reproducibility is not guaranteed across versions/hardware even with deterministic algorithms enabled.

### G. Prompt-injection taxonomies & the lethal trifecta
- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025** still ranks Prompt Injection as **LLM01:2025** ("occurs when user prompts alter the behavior of the LLM, resulting in data leakage, privilege escalation, or unwanted actions"); **LLM06:2025 Excessive Agency** ("the vulnerability that enables damaging actions to be performed in response to unexpected, ambiguous or manipulated outputs from an LLM") is the other directly relevant entry. OWASP's GenAI Security Project also released **"Agentic AI – Threats and Mitigations"** (Feb 2025) and the **"Top 10 for Agentic Applications" / "Top 10 Risks & Mitigations for Agentic AI"** (released Dec 10, 2025, using ASI-prefixed identifiers distinct from the LLM Top 10).
- **The lethal trifecta** — Simon Willison, "The lethal trifecta for AI agents: private data, untrusted content, and external communication" (June 16, 2025): "Access to your private data... Exposure to untrusted content... The ability to externally communicate in a way that could be used to steal your data." **Meta's "Agents Rule of Two: A Practical Approach to AI Agent Security"** (Oct 31, 2025) operationalizes it: "agents must satisfy no more than two of the following three properties within a session — [A] process untrustworthy inputs, [B] access sensitive systems/private data, [C] change state or communicate externally." Notably, Willison himself proposes the exact **"taint tracking + policy gating"** deterministic mitigation (carry `reads_private_data` / `sees_untrusted_content` / `can_exfiltrate` metadata on tools and block all-three in one tainted path) that Verity is. AgentDojo is the eval that best operationalizes the trifecta as reusable cases.
- **Indirect-injection data beyond InjecAgent:** **BIPIA** (Microsoft) is the major addition — 5 application scenarios (email/web/table/summarization/code QA) × 50 attack types (~86k prompts), with a BIPIA-Clean benign counterpart. License nuance: the BIPIA *code/instruction set* is permissive (reported MIT for the instruction dataset), but the summarization/WebQA *context data* must be regenerated locally due to upstream licensing; a derivative 70k-example HF dataset is CC BY-SA 4.0.

### H. Honest gaps & contrarian evidence
- **Simulated tools miss real-execution failure modes.** RAS-Eval and similar argue real execution captures state-transition vulnerabilities static simulation cannot. Your deferred real-execution tier is reasonable, but document it as a known coverage gap.
- **Deterministic oracles miss semantic harm.** A state-diff oracle catches "secret file was read" but not "model wrote a subtly poisoned config." This is the inherent trade you accept for reproducibility; pair narrow LLM sub-checks where unavoidable.
- **Matched controls may be insufficient for FP estimation.** InjecGuard shows over-defense is driven by trigger-word bias; a handful of matched controls per attack may under-sample the benign space. Add a larger NotInject-style benign pool.
- **Small-model results may not generalize** across quant levels and chat templates; pin and report both.
- **Genuine voids you must author:** (1) the self-modification/persistence class; (2) **benign-user-hallucination catastrophe cases** — every existing "harmful action" corpus assumes either indirect injection or a malicious user; none model a *benign user + hallucinating small model* reaching a catastrophic action; (3) a unified three-valued (attempted-violation / safe / invalid) scoring schema that normalizes ASR over valid-only AND all — nothing existing reports this cleanly.

### Critique of the 8 design choices

1. **Small models (1-3B) first.** **Supported.** BFCL + the TinyLLM edge-device study (arXiv:2511.22138) show steep small-model degradation, especially multi-turn (Qwen3-1.7B 16.88%, Qwen3-0.6B 1.38%) and schema adherence. As agents proliferate to local/edge, this is a growing surface. Keep frontier as accuracy anchor.
2. **Simulated, side-effect-free tools.** **Supported with a documented caveat.** Reproducible and safe; matches AgentDojo/τ-bench/ToolEmu practice. But RAS-Eval-type evidence says real execution exposes state-transition bugs simulation misses — log this as a known gap and keep the deferred real-execution tier on the roadmap.
3. **Deterministic code oracles, no LLM judge for the verdict.** **Strongly supported.** Judge-reliability literature (Judge Reliability Harness arXiv:2603.05399; "Justice or Prejudice?") shows no judge is uniformly reliable; deterministic scoring is bias-immune. AgentDojo, τ-bench, InterCode all do effect-based scoring. Narrow LLM sub-checks for benign-task-completion are acceptable (prefer 0-5 scale, per arXiv:2601.03444).
4. **Matched benign controls (over-blocking = #1 metric).** **Supported.** AgentHarm (matched pairs + refusal/harm separation) and InjecGuard/NotInject (over-defense measurement, 339 benign trigger-word samples) are direct precedents. Caveat: consider a larger benign pool than 1:1 to sample the trigger-word space.
5. **Capability/intent separation; three-valued outcome; ASR over valid-only AND all.** **Supported and best-practice.** AgentHarm separates refusal from incapability; ASB and others report PNA (performance-no-attack) and refusal rate. The valid-vs-all normalization is a known pitfall — reporting both is exactly right and currently rare.
6. **Authored catastrophe floor (secret-read/shell/self-mod).** **Supported as a genuine gap.** Nothing covers these with crisp labels for a benign-user model. Seed from NL2Bash/InterCode (MIT), gitleaks (MIT), GTFOBins/LOLBAS. Self-mod is essentially greenfield.
7. **Minimal hand-written ReAct harness with dispatch chokepoint.** **Supported.** No single framework fits all four needs; a thin loop with the dispatch step as the future verifier insertion point is the cleanest design. Borrow Inspect's structure/logs; consider smolagents for the loop implementation.
8. **Reproducibility (pinned revision/quant/seed, hashed corpus, manifests; honest about greedy non-bit-exactness).** **Supported and correctly hedged.** He/Thinking Machines proved temp-0 ≠ bit-exact under batching (80 unique completions from 1,000 runs). Claim "reproducible up to documented batch-nondeterminism." Optionally offer a batch-invariant-kernel mode for a stricter reproducibility tier (~34-60% throughput cost).

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt AgentDojo as the spine and InjecAgent + BIPIA for breadth** — all permissively licensed, all loadable as data. Replace InjecAgent's tool-name oracle with your effect-based oracle.
2. **Build the catastrophe floor by seeding, not from scratch:** NL2Bash + corrected InterCode-Bash (MIT) for shell; gitleaks default ruleset (MIT) as the `is_secret` oracle; GTFOBins/LOLBAS for dangerous-binary and persistence patterns. Avoid vendoring AGPL trufflehog.
3. **Steal the InterCode/τ-bench container-state-diff oracle pattern** (git-diff + file-hash + DB-state-vs-goal) for your shell-exec and exfiltration effect oracles — it is the most battle-tested deterministic agent oracle.
4. **Adopt AgentHarm's matched-pair + refusal/harm-separation methodology**, and add an InjecGuard/NotInject-style benign trigger-word pool to harden your over-blocking (#1) metric against trigger-word bias.
5. **Keep your hand-written ReAct loop but adopt Inspect's conventions** (dataset→Task→Solver→Scorer, structured logs, sandboxing toolkit, tool-approval hooks). Use smolagents as the agent-under-test scaffold if you want a ready ReAct/tool-calling implementation; instrument tools so your code oracle inspects every dispatch — and isolate execution in Docker/E2B (smolagents' local executor is not a security boundary).
6. **Structure your attack taxonomy on the lethal trifecta / Meta's Rule of Two and OWASP LLM01 + LLM06** — this gives reviewers a recognized framework and maps 1:1 to Verity's taint-gating design (Willison's own proposed mitigation).
7. **Adopt a reproducibility manifest** (model revision + quant hash + seed + corpus content-hash + vLLM version + batch config) and report "reproducible up to documented batch-nondeterminism." Offer an optional batch-invariant-kernel tier for users who need bit-exactness.
8. **Author the two true voids explicitly:** (a) benign-user-hallucination catastrophe cases, and (b) the self-modification/persistence class. Frame these as Verity's novel contribution.
9. **Report ASR three ways** (over valid-only, over all, and refusal-rate) as standard output; document the valid-vs-all normalization choice per run.
10. **Calibrate any narrow LLM sub-check against a human-labeled holdout** (R-Judge-style, but author your own to avoid the CC BY-NC-SA license); use a 0-5 scale and track agreement, and never let a sub-check feed the catastrophe verdict.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that would change these recommendations:** if a real-execution agent-safety corpus with a permissive license and catastrophe-floor coverage appears (watch RAS-Eval and OWASP ASI artifacts); if small-model BFCL-V4 agentic numbers show >90% multi-turn reliability at 1-3B (would weaken the "small models are the threat" framing — currently they sit near 1-17% multi-turn); or if a validated deterministic semantic-harm oracle emerges (would let you narrow the LLM sub-check surface further).

## Caveats
- **Licenses must be re-verified at integration time.** Confirmed: NL2Bash, InterCode, gitleaks (MIT); BIPIA code (MIT, with some context data regenerated locally); Inspect, promptfoo, PyRIT, lm-eval, OpenAI Evals (MIT); HELM, smolagents, garak (Apache-2.0); trufflehog (AGPL-3.0); R-Judge (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, non-commercial). I could **not** independently verify exact LICENSE files for ToolEmu, ASB, SafeAgentBench, ToolSword, GTFOBins, and LOLBAS — check each repo before redistribution.
- **Some figures are from secondary sources or single studies** (the TinyLLM/BFCL small-model numbers; the >50% InterCode error rate; ASB's 84.30% peak ASR). Treat as directional, not definitive, and reproduce on your own stack.
- **The promptfoo→OpenAI acquisition (reported March 2026)** comes from secondary sources only; verify governance/license continuity before deep adoption.
- **"30-80% format failure" for small-model tool calling** is consistent with BFCL multi-turn trends but is a range, not a single measured constant; report your own measured rates.
- **Lower-confidence area:** my final planned searches on garak/PyRIT head-to-head and benchmark-contamination detection were cut off by search-budget exhaustion. The harness facts were gathered via a dedicated research subagent with primary-source URLs (high confidence), but the data-contamination guidance is inferred from general eval-reproducibility literature rather than an agent-specific leakage study — treat contamination handling as an open item to validate. Because Verity adapts public benchmarks (AgentDojo, InjecAgent, BIPIA) and runs open models that may have trained on them, prudent mitigations regardless: hold out a private slice, perturb surface forms of seeded cases, and track per-source performance deltas as a leakage signal.
